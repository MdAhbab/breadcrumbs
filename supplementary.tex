\section{Reproducing every number}

All measurements in the main report come from benchmarks that write JSON, which a
generator turns into \LaTeX{} macros. No figure is typed by hand, and a figure
that has not been measured renders as a bold \textbf{??} on the page. The
measurements quoted were taken on \num{LedgerMachine}.

\begin{verbatim}
cd "Breadcrumbs Project"
make setup            # virtual environment and dependencies

make test             # 206 tests
make lint             # ruff, clean
make demo             # the end-to-end cycle
make verify           # chain integrity from scratch

# the mechanisms, individually
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -k "accumulator or seal or witness or digest" -v

# the attacks: this should read as a list of refusals, plus three admissions
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -k "attack" -v

# the measurements
.venv/bin/python -m model.bench.bench_accumulator --bits 3072
.venv/bin/python -m model.bench.bench_identity
.venv/bin/python -m model.bench.bench_ledger --documents 200
.venv/bin/python -m model.bench.to_latex     # writes results.tex

# the original learning simulation, unchanged since the first submission
cd model/experiments
python3 fcl_sim.py            # Table 4 and Figure 3
python3 sweep_difficulty.py   # the difficulty sweep
python3 probe_sufficiency.py  # the test in Section 7.3 that we failed
\end{verbatim}

Two notes on honesty of reproduction. The learning experiments in
\texttt{model/experiments/} are frozen and lint-exempt precisely so that the
numbers they back cannot drift without somebody noticing. And the test suite
draws RSA keys from a pre-generated development pool in
\texttt{model/ledger/devkeys.pem} --- those private keys are public, every
signature check in the suite is nonetheless a real RSA-PSS verification against a
real certificate chain, and what is pre-computed is only the search for large
primes.

\subsection{The modulus ceremony}

The accumulator modulus is generated by a \textbf{trusted dealer} from combined
member entropy. Each member contributes secret bytes and publishes only their
hash; the transcript --- dealer, contributors, contribution hashes, parameter hash
and a note stating the limitation in plain words --- is stored on the ledger in
full rather than by digest, because a member joining in year three needs to read
who was in the room, and a hash of a document nobody kept is a gesture.

This is not multiparty generation. A dishonest dealer retains a trapdoor. Two
alternatives exist and both cost something we were not willing to pay yet.
Distributed generation in the style of Boneh--Franklin removes the dealer but is a
research protocol. A class group of imaginary quadratic order has no trapdoor at
all, but is roughly an order of magnitude slower and stops being RSA, which
removes the unification that motivates the whole design. The mitigation we chose
instead is structural and is tested: the accumulator is never the sole authority,
so a trapdoor holder can fail a verification but cannot produce the ledger record
and the anchored index that verification also requires.

\section{The second-generation corpus}

The corpus behind the learning results is invented, and its weakness is stated in
the main report: a model trained only on summaries of it does nearly as well as
the full method, so the benchmark cannot separate ``the mechanism works'' from
``this data is easy to summarise''. A second corpus is specified to address that,
and to address something the first corpus cannot do at all.

\textbf{Row-level records.} Five document types with raw rows rather than
pre-extracted features, so a detector has to learn from the records instead of
from sixteen hand-designed statistics. This is what makes the summary-sufficiency
criticism answerable either way.

\textbf{A labelled adversary trace.} This is the part without which the ledger
cannot be scored at all, only asserted. Alongside the documents, a timeline of
attempted attacks with ground truth: retroactive edits, records withheld at
disclosure, back-dated seals, forked histories served to different members,
witness collusion, duplicate submissions to two buyer channels, and factories that
hide a real change inside a high volume of trivial amendments. Every attack has a
difficulty parameter and a configuration in which the attacker is \emph{careful}
--- edits that preserve totals, withholding that preserves counts, amendments that
look routine. An adversary that always leaves obvious traces flatters the system
being tested, and a reviewer will assume that is what happened unless the
generator shows otherwise.

\textbf{Structure.} Six invented sites differing in size, wage level,
record-keeping discipline and seasonality; a non-IID partition across them
(Dirichlet, $\alpha = 0.6$); three waves of new anomaly types across 2025--2027
with drift inside each wave; and a deliberate recurrence of wave-one anomalies
late in wave three, so forgetting becomes measurable in production terms rather
than only against a held-out benchmark.

\textbf{Acceptance tests the generator must pass}, including one that matters more
than it looks: a trivial baseline must score well short of perfect on the
realistic mix. A synthetic corpus that a logistic regression solves makes every
result computed on it meaningless, and it is the first thing a reviewer will try.

The full specification is in \texttt{docs/synthetic\_corpus\_prompt.md} in the
repository.

\section{Security audit findings}

The system was audited before this report was revised. Five serious findings, all
fixed, each with a regression test that fails against the previous code.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Audit findings. The two critical ones each made the project's headline
guarantee false while the demonstration appeared to work perfectly.}
\label{tab:audit}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l l Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{\#} & \textbf{Severity} & \textbf{Finding} \\
\midrule
F-1 & Critical & Endorsement signatures verified against a key the endorsement itself supplied, so any party could satisfy any policy alone \\
F-2 & Critical & The same flaw in the Continuity Gate: one actor could forge every organisation's evaluation and promote a model that had collapsed from 91.6\% to 52.9\% on an earlier task \\
F-3 & High & No transaction replay protection; a blind write could be applied twice \\
F-4 & High & Blocks could mix channels, breaking the confidentiality boundary the whole Plane A argument rests on \\
F-5 & High & The API served every factory record to any signed-in caller, including the regulator, whose screen exists to show no factory data \\
F-6 & Medium & The certificate expiry check compared against the issuance epoch and was unreachable \\
F-7 & Medium & One hardcoded timestamp for every write, making grant expiry meaningless \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Two gaps the audit recorded as open have since been closed: range queries now
carry a digest of the keys the scan returned, so a phantom read cannot corrupt a
period seal, and epoch digests give the external cross-check a single-store
deployment previously lacked. Blocks are still not signed by the ordering service
and clients still do not sign their own proposals.

The lesson the audit drew is the one worth repeating: both critical findings were
in signature verification, both looked correct on a casual read, and neither would
have been caught by any test that only exercises the happy path. Every guarantee
in this system now has a test that names an attacker and tries the attack.
